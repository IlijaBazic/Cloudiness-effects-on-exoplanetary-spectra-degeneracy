### Key Literature References for Methodology & Physical Interpretation

1. **Cloud & Haze Muting Effect on Transmission Spectra:**
   * **Sing, D. K., et al. (2016).** *A continuum from clear to cloudy hot-Jupiter atmospheres.* **Nature**, 529(7584), 59-62.
     > *Justification:* Demonstrates that aerosols and cloud decks systematically mute spectral absorption features, suppressing thermal and abundance variations in transit spectroscopy.

2. **3D Atmospheric Asymmetries & Thermal-Cloud Degeneracy:**
   * **Line, M. R., & Parmentier, V. (2016).** *The influence of non-uniform cloud cover on transit transmission spectra.* **The Astrophysical Journal**, 820(1), 78.
     > *Justification:* Shows that partial cloud coverage (asymmetry between morning/evening terminators) dominates the transmission signal and causes strong degeneracies with atmospheric temperature profiles.

3. **Transmission Asymmetry Metrics & JWST Observational Framework:**
   * **Espinoza, N., & Jones, K. (2021).** *Constraining 3D Exoplanet Atmospheres from Transmission Spectra.* **The Astrophysical Journal**, 914(1), 12.
     > *Justification:* Establishes the mathematical framework for calculating asymmetry indices ($AI$) and retrieval biased by inhomogeneous terminator properties.

4. **WASP-17b Aerosol & Terminator Diagnostics:**
   * **Grant, D., et al. (2023).** *JWST NIRSpec Transmission Spectroscopy of WASP-17b.* **Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society**, 525(2), 2840-2856.
     > *Justification:* Direct reference for WASP-17b's extended, low-density atmosphere where hazes and terminator asymmetries play a critical role in HST/JWST transmission data.
